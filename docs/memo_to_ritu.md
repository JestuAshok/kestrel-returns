Subject: Returns: call the risky orders, don't hold them

THE DECISION
Before dispatch, phone the customer on the orders our model rates riskiest: about 3 in 10 orders (roughly 210 a month, around 10 calls a day). Do not hold them. Shield members get the same call, never a hold.

THE NUMBER
We cannot honestly promise 95% accuracy. Only about 11 in 100 orders come back, so a model that says "nothing will return" is already 89% right. Ours reaches about 89.5%. Its strength is ranking: among the 10% of orders it rates riskiest, about 4 in 10 come back, against 1 in 9 overall. Still, about 1 in 4 of the orders we would call really come back. The model narrows down where to call; it cannot say which single order will return. On eight months of past orders it had not seen, the ranking was steady.

THE RUPEES
Each return costs Rs 1,150 (Finance's figure), not Rs 600. At 700 orders a month, returns cost about Rs 91,000. Calling about 210 orders costs about Rs 9,500 and saves about Rs 21,000. Net gain: about Rs 11,000 to 11,500 a month, roughly Rs 1.4 lakh a year (range Rs 9,700 to 13,300; worst month tested +Rs 8,650). Holding does not pay: about 12% of held customers cancel, and most would not have returned the order, so we lose the sale. The model costs Rs 0 per order to run.
Biggest assumption: the 35% effect of a call comes from the spring pilot. Below about 16% the saving disappears; at 20% it is still about Rs 2,500 a month.
